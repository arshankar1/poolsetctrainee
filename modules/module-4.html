1. Chemical Dosing Fundamentals

Dosing is not guesswork. Every chemical addition should be based on: (1) the current reading, (2) the target reading, and (3) the pool volume. Under-dosing wastes time and money. Over-dosing harms the water and can be dangerous.

2. Chemical Safety Rules

🚨 THE BIG RED RULE — Never Forget This
NEVER add granular shock directly to the pool, skimmer, or any concentrated form.

Always pre-dissolve shock in a bucket of water first. Add the chemical to water — never add water to the chemical. This applies to cal-hypo, dichlor, and any granular oxidizer.

Rules That Cannot Be Broken

  • ⛔ Never mix shock and acid. Chlorine + muriatic acid = toxic chlorine gas. These must never be stored together, added to the pool at the same time, or mixed in any container.
  • ⛔ Always add chemical to water. When pre-dissolving shock in a bucket, put water in first, then slowly add the shock. Adding water to shock can cause violent splashing or ignition.
  • ⚠️ Never add acid to the skimmer. Concentrated acid in pipes near the pump causes corrosion and equipment damage.
  • ⚠️ Add CYA through the skimmer in a diluted sock. CYA dissolves slowly — place it in an old sock or nylon stocking in the skimmer basket to allow gradual dissolution. Don't backwash for 48 hours.

3. Phosphates — Algae's Food Supply

Phosphates enter pools from fertilizer runoff, decaying leaves, swimmer waste, and some fill water. They don't directly harm the water — but they're the primary food source for algae. A pool with high phosphates will be much harder to keep algae-free, even with proper chlorine levels.

Threshold: Act when phosphates exceed 200 ppb (parts per billion). Use a phosphate remover product — it precipitates phosphates out of solution. The water may temporarily become milky — this is normal and will clear.

Strategy: When a pool keeps getting algae despite correct chemistry, test for phosphates. High phosphates are often the hidden culprit.

Equipment & Filtration

Identify every component on an equipment pad, understand the three filter types, know when and how to backwash, and recognize equipment problems before they become failures.

⚙️
By the end of this module you will be able to:
  • Identify all major components on a residential equipment pad
  • Explain the three filter types and how to clean each one
  • Read a pressure gauge and know when to backwash
  • Recognize common equipment problems and how to document them

1. Equipment Pad Components

Every residential pool has an equipment pad — the area where all mechanical and filtration components are located. You'll inspect this at every service stop. Knowing what each part does lets you spot problems early.

🔄 Pump
The heart of the system. Pulls water from the pool via the skimmer and main drain, pushes it through the filter, and returns it via return jets. Listen for rattling (debris in impeller), grinding (bearing failure), or cavitation (air in lines).
🗑️ Strainer Basket
Located in the pump housing. Catches large debris before it reaches the impeller. Clean at every visit — a clogged basket starves the pump of water, causing it to run hot and fail prematurely.
🔵 Filter
Removes fine particles from the water. Three types: sand, D.E., and cartridge. Each cleaned differently. Monitor the pressure gauge — rising pressure means the filter needs cleaning.
📊 Pressure Gauge
Measures water pressure inside the filter. Note the "clean" baseline (typically 10–15 PSI). When pressure rises 8–10 PSI above baseline, it's time to clean the filter.
🔥 Heater
Heats pool water for comfort. Gas or heat pump. Check for unusual noises, error codes on the display, and proper ignition. Low water flow through the heater can cause damage.
⏰ Timeclock
Controls when the pump runs. Verify the on/off pins are set correctly at every visit — an incorrect timeclock setting is a common cause of water chemistry failures.

2. The Three Filter Types

Filter Type Filtration Cleaning Method After Cleaning
Sand ~20 microns Backwash, then Rinse Return to Filter position
D.E. ~2 microns (finest) Backwash, then add fresh D.E. Add D.E. through skimmer
Cartridge ~10 microns Remove, rinse with hose Reinstall, no backwash
⛔ Never backwash a cartridge filter. There is no backwash valve on cartridge filters. Remove the element, rinse it from top to bottom between the pleats with a garden hose. Soak in filter cleaner solution monthly or quarterly.

3. When to Backwash

Backwash when the pressure gauge reads 8–10 PSI above the clean baseline. Most filters start at 10–15 PSI when clean. At 20–25 PSI, flow is severely restricted and the filter is no longer cleaning the water effectively.

Backwash Process (Sand Filter)

  1. Turn pump OFF
  2. Turn multiport valve to BACKWASH
  3. Turn pump ON — run until waste water runs clear (30–90 seconds)
  4. Turn pump OFF again
  5. Turn valve to RINSE — run for 15–30 seconds
  6. Turn pump OFF
  7. Turn valve back to FILTER
  8. Turn pump ON — check new pressure reading (should be back to baseline)
Always run Rinse after backwashing. Skipping the Rinse step sends disturbed sand into the pool, causing cloudiness. 15–30 seconds is enough.

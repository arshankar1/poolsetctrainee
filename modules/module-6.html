1. The SEC Greeting Formula

Every time you see a customer — arriving at a stop, leaving a stop, passing them in the yard — you have 7 seconds to make or reinforce an impression. SEC is the three-move formula that makes every encounter positive.

S
Smile
+
E
Eye Contact
+
C
Comment

The Comment is the most important and often forgotten piece. It's not just "Hi." It's a personal, relevant remark: "Hi Mrs. Garcia, the pool is looking crystal clear today" or "Good morning, I noticed your roses are blooming — beautiful." It shows you're present, engaged, and human — not just a worker.

First impressions: Studies show people form an opinion in about 7 seconds. Your posture, eye contact, smile, and opening words create an impression that is very hard to undo. Make every 7 seconds count.

2. Handling Complaints

At some point, a customer will be unhappy. How you respond in those moments defines your relationship far more than a perfect service visit. The right approach turns complaints into loyalty.

The 4-Step Complaint Model: LAUA

👂
1. Listen
Let them finish. Don't interrupt. Don't defend.
🤝
2. Acknowledge
"I hear you, and I understand why you're frustrated."
🔍
3. Understand
Ask clarifying questions. Find out exactly what happened.
✅
4. Act
Tell them exactly what you'll do and follow through.
Never defend first. Even if you know you're right, defending yourself before acknowledging the customer's feelings immediately puts them on the defensive. Acknowledge first — explain later.
Example LAUA conversation:

Customer: "The pool has been cloudy for two weeks. I don't feel like I'm getting what I'm paying for."

❌ Bad response: "We were just out there Tuesday and it looked fine. It might be the weather."

✅ Good LAUA response:
"I hear you, and I completely understand why that's frustrating." (Listen + Acknowledge)
"Can you tell me — was it cloudy right after we visited, or did it come on a day or two later?" (Understand)
"Got it. I'm going to run a full test right now and show you the numbers so we can figure out exactly what's going on — and I'll make sure we fix it today." (Act)

3. Satisfied vs. Raving Fan

😐 Satisfied Customer

  • Pool is clean, they pay the bill
  • Stays quiet — doesn't complain, but doesn't promote
  • May cancel at first sign of trouble
  • Neutral ambassador for your business

⭐ Raving Fan

  • Actively tells neighbors, friends, and family
  • Leaves 5-star reviews without being asked
  • Defends you when something goes wrong
  • Stays loyal through price increases and mistakes

The goal isn't to avoid complaints — it's to exceed expectations so consistently that customers can't imagine using anyone else. Proactive service (noticing the loose equipment cover before they do, mentioning the water is looking a bit hazy before they complain) is the #1 creator of raving fans.

Real-world example:

Imagine two pool techs. Both clean pools equally well.

Tech A finishes the job, leaves without a word.
Tech B finishes, then knocks on the door: "Just wanted to let you know — the pool looks great today. I did notice your timer was set to turn off at 4 PM, which is earlier than ideal for summer. I adjusted it to 7 PM so you get full circulation. Should make a difference in keeping it clear."

Which tech does that customer call when their neighbor asks for a pool service recommendation? Which one are they going to leave a 5-star review for? Which one keeps the account even if there's a price increase?

That's the difference between a satisfied customer and a raving fan.

1. The Three Elements of Pool Care

Every healthy pool requires three things working together. Neglect any one and the others compensate less and less until the pool fails. Think of it as a three-legged stool.

⚗️ Chemistry

Balanced chemistry keeps the water safe for swimmers and protects equipment. The key parameters are pH, total alkalinity, calcium hardness, free chlorine, and CYA. When chemistry is off, everything else suffers — cloudy water, algae, scale, corrosion.

pH is the most important single parameter. It directly controls how effective your chlorine is. An ideal pH of 7.2–7.6 means roughly 50–60% of your chlorine is active. At pH 8.0, only ~20% is active — so you can have "enough" chlorine on paper but almost none doing work.

🔄 Circulation

The pump pulls water through the skimmer and main drain, pushes it through the filter, and returns it to the pool through the return jets. Without circulation, chemistry treatments can't reach the whole pool, dead zones form, and algae blooms in corners and steps.

Run the pump 8–12 hours per day in summer. The timeclock controls this — check it is set correctly at every service stop. One full "turnover" of pool volume through the filter per day is the minimum goal.

🧹 Maintenance

Physical maintenance removes debris, biofilm, and sediment that chemistry alone can't handle. Skimming removes floating leaves, brushing dislodges biofilm from walls before it becomes visible algae, and vacuuming removes settled debris from the floor.

Even chemically-balanced pools with good circulation will develop problems without regular mechanical cleaning. An ounce of brushing is worth a pound of algaecide.

2. Key Chemistry Ranges

You'll test these parameters at every service stop. Knowing the ranges cold — without looking them up — is essential to making quick, correct chemical decisions in the field.

Parameter Ideal Range Too Low Too High
pH 7.2 – 7.6 Corrosive, eye irritation Kills chlorine, scaling
Total Alkalinity 80 – 120 ppm pH bounce, corrosion Cloudy water, scaling
Calcium Hardness 200 – 400 ppm Plaster etching Scaling on surfaces
Free Chlorine 1 – 3 ppm Algae, bacteria, illness Bleaches swimwear, irritates
CYA (Stabilizer) 30 – 50 ppm Chlorine burns off in sun Chlorine lock, ineffective FC
Memory trick: "pH is mood, alkalinity is the bodyguard." When alkalinity is right, pH stays stable. When alkalinity is low, pH bounces around wildly with every chemical addition — like a mood without a bodyguard.

3. CYA — The Double-Edged Stabilizer

Cyanuric acid (CYA) is the stabilizer in trichlor tablets and most granular chlorine products. It forms a temporary bond with chlorine molecules, shielding them from UV light. Without CYA, outdoor chlorine evaporates in 2–4 hours of direct sun.

But CYA accumulates. Every time you add trichlor tabs, you're adding more CYA. Over time, pools can reach 100–200 ppm — this is called over-stabilization. At these levels, the CYA holds the chlorine so tightly that it can't kill bacteria or algae effectively. The pool may "test fine" for chlorine but still turn green. There is no chemical that removes CYA — the only solution is to drain and dilute.

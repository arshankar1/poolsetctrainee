1. The 8-Step Service Visit

Every service stop follows the same sequence — no exceptions. The order exists for good reasons: each step prepares for the next, and doing things out of order creates more work or makes the pool worse.

Why add chemicals LAST? Adding chemicals before or during cleaning stirs up debris that then absorbs the chemicals unevenly. Brushing after shocking can scatter partially-dissolved shock and cause bleaching. Chemicals need a clean, settled pool to distribute properly.

2. Brushing Technique

Brushing is non-negotiable — even when the pool looks clean. Algae begins as a microscopic biofilm on pool surfaces, invisible to the naked eye. By the time it's visible (green), it's already a major problem. Regular brushing breaks the biofilm before it can establish.

Brush Selection by Surface Type

Plaster/Gunite
Combination nylon/stainless steel brush. The steel bristles scrub rough surfaces; nylon protects grout lines.
Vinyl Liner
Soft nylon bristles ONLY. Steel bristles will puncture or tear vinyl liner — a very expensive mistake.
Fiberglass
Soft nylon bristles. Fiberglass can scratch easily — never use stainless steel on fiberglass pools.

Brushing order: Start at the waterline and work down. Brush walls, then steps, then the floor edges. Use long, overlapping strokes. Push sediment toward the main drain.

3. Vacuuming Technique

Vacuuming removes sediment that has settled to the pool floor after brushing. Done correctly, it leaves the pool spotless. Done rushed, it just stirs the sediment back up.

  • Move slowly — moving too fast lifts sediment into the water instead of vacuuming it
  • Overlap 25–30% on every pass — ensures no spots are missed
  • Straight passes from one end to the other — systematic, not random
  • Start in the deep end and work toward the shallow — gravity helps settle disturbed sediment
  • Watch the pressure gauge — if pressure drops, the vacuum hose or filter is clogging
